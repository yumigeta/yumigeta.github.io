How Raman Spectroscopy Works

Raman scattering reveals the vibrational fingerprint of a material — a workhorse technique in 2D materials and condensed matter physics. The key rule: a vibration is Raman-active only if it changes the molecule's polarizability. Explore that link below, then see the quantum energy-level picture.

01 — From molecular vibration to the Raman signal

How a vibration modulates the polarizability

Pick a normal mode of CO2. The molecule vibrates on the left, and the electron cloud (its polarizability α) breathes in sync on the right. That changing α modulates the dipole induced by the laser field, p(t) = α(t)·E(t) — and the modulation creates the Stokes and anti-Stokes sidebands. Everything below shares one clock: the vertical “now” line marks the instant shown by the molecule.

02 — Quantum picture

Energy-level (Jablonski) diagram

A photon excites the molecule to a short-lived virtual state, then a photon is scattered as it relaxes. Where it lands sets the scattered energy: same level (Rayleigh), a higher vibrational level (Stokes, red-shifted), or back down from an excited level (anti-Stokes, blue-shifted).
