Graphene Electronic Structure

The honeycomb lattice, Brillouin zone, tight-binding band structure — visualizing how Dirac cones emerge at the K points and give graphene its massless charge carriers.

00 — Crystal structure

Honeycomb lattice & Brillouin zone

Graphene's honeycomb is built from two interpenetrating triangular sublattices (A and B). The unit cell contains exactly two atoms. In reciprocal space, the first Brillouin zone is a hexagon whose corners — the K and K′ points — are where the Dirac physics emerges.

Left: Real-space honeycomb with lattice vectors a1, a2 and the unit cell (dashed). Right: First Brillouin zone with high-symmetry points Γ, M, K, K′ and the path Γ→M→K→Γ.

01 — Band structure

E-k dispersion along high-symmetry path

In the nearest-neighbor tight-binding model, the energy bands are E(k) = ±t |f(k)|, where t ≈ 2.7 eV is the hopping energy between adjacent carbon pz orbitals. The conduction (π*) and valence (π) bands touch at the K point with zero gap — graphene is a zero-gap semiconductor.

At the K point the bands touch linearly: E ≈ ±ℏvF|k−K|, giving massless Dirac fermions with Fermi velocity vF ≈ 106 m/s.
02 — 3D Dirac cones

Full band structure over the Brillouin zone

The complete E(kx, ky) surface reveals the conical band touching points — the Dirac cones — at each K and K′ corner of the hexagonal Brillouin zone. Drag to rotate.

drag to rotate · scroll to zoom
The energy surface has six Dirac cones — three at K and three at K′ — connected by saddle points at M. The bandwidth is 6t (from −3t at Γ in the valence band to +3t at Γ in the conduction band).
03 — Quantum confinement

From graphene to nanoribbons: discretized bands

Cut graphene into a narrow nanoribbon and the electrons become confined across its width: the transverse momentum is quantized, so only a discrete family of parallel cutting lines survives in the Brillouin zone. The orientation of those lines is set by the edge geometry — zigzag (θ = 0°) or armchair (θ = 30°) — and their spacing by the width N. Whether any line passes through a Dirac point decides if the ribbon is metallic.

Left: top-down view of the Brillouin zone with the family of cutting lines at the chosen edge orientation. Right: the same lines laid over the full tight-binding band surface (drag to rotate, scroll to zoom). A line through a Dirac point (corner) is gapless; the nearest one otherwise sets the gap.
