About

I am a researcher in the Department of Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Arizona, working on the physics and applications of two-dimensional layered materials and quantum heterostructures.

My work combines advanced synthesis techniques with optical and electronic characterization to explore emergent quantum phenomena in atomically thin crystals. I have contributed to the discovery of generalized Wigner crystal states in transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) moire superlattices and developed new approaches for controlling the properties of rare-earth tritelluride quantum materials.

Previously, I was at Arizona State University in the Tongay Research Group, where I worked extensively on the synthesis and characterization of van der Waals layered materials.

Research

My research explores the fundamental physics and practical applications of low-dimensional quantum materials.

01

Rare-Earth Tritelluride Quantum Materials

Investigating the structure, synthesis, and emergent properties (charge density waves, superconductivity) of the RTe3 family across the full lanthanide series, including their phononic behavior, surface oxidation, and environmental stability.

02

Moire Superlattices & Correlated Electron States

Studying Mott insulating states and generalized Wigner crystals in WSe2/WS2 moire superlattices using optical spectroscopy and scanning probe techniques for real-space imaging of correlated electron phases.

03

2D Semiconductor Synthesis & Device Fabrication

Developing scalable methods for synthesis, transfer, and device integration of atomically thin semiconductors including MoTe2, GeSe, and graphene nanoribbons, with applications in single-photon emission and nanoelectronics.
