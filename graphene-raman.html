Graphene Raman Spectroscopyグラフェンのラマン分光

Interactive guideインタラクティブガイド

Raman spectroscopy is the go-to tool for characterizing graphene: it identifies the number of layers, quantifies defects, and measures strain and doping — all from a quick, non-destructive optical measurement.ラマン分光法はグラフェンの評価に最も広く使われる手法です。層数の同定、欠陥の定量、そしてひずみとドーピングの測定を、迅速かつ非破壊な光学測定だけで行えます。

00 — Phonon dispersion00 — フォノン分散

Six phonon branches from two atoms2つの原子から生まれる6本のフォノンブランチ

Graphene's unit cell has two carbon atoms, giving rise to six phonon branches: three acoustic (A) and three optical (O). Each is further classified as in-plane longitudinal (L), in-plane transverse (T), or out-of-plane (Z). The Raman-active modes live near the $\Gamma$ point (first-order) and the K point (double resonance).グラフェンの単位胞には2つの炭素原子があり、 6本のフォノンブランチ（音響3本(A)と光学3本(O)）が生じます。 それぞれは面内縦波(L)、面内横波(T)、面外(Z)に分類されます。ラマン活性モードは $\Gamma$ 点（一次過程）およびK点（二重共鳴）付近に存在します。

Phonon dispersion of graphene along $\Gamma \to M \to K \to \Gamma$. The iTO branch near K (~1350 cm−1) gives rise to the D and 2D bands. The iLO and iTO branches at Γ (~1580 cm−1) produce the G band. Hover over the branches to identify them.$\Gamma \to M \to K \to \Gamma$ に沿ったグラフェンのフォノン分散。K点付近のiTOブランチ（~1350 cm−1）がDバンドと2Dバンドを生み出します。Γ点のiLOとiTOブランチ（~1580 cm−1）がGバンドを生成します。ブランチにカーソルを合わせると名前が表示されます。

01 — The G band01 — Gバンド

First-order Raman scattering at ~1580 cm−1~1580 cm−1 における一次ラマン散乱

The G band is the only first-order Raman peak in graphene. It originates from the doubly degenerate $E_{2g}$ phonon mode at the Brillouin zone center ($\Gamma$ point), where adjacent carbon atoms vibrate against each other in-plane.Gバンドはグラフェンにおける唯一の一次ラマンピークです。ブリルアンゾーンの中心（$\Gamma$ 点）における二重縮退した $E_{2g}$ フォノンモードに由来し、隣接する炭素原子が面内で互いに逆方向に振動します。

$E_{2g}$ vibration at $\Gamma$$\Gamma$ 点の $E_{2g}$ 振動

Scattering process散乱過程

First-order process: an incident photon creates one phonon at $\Gamma$ ($\mathbf{q} \approx 0$) and is scattered. Because this is a zone-center mode, the G band frequency (~1580 cm−1) is independent of laser wavelength. The G band is present in all sp2 carbon materials — graphene, graphite, carbon nanotubes, and amorphous carbon.一次過程：入射光子が $\Gamma$ 点（$\mathbf{q} \approx 0$）で1つのフォノンを生成し散乱されます。ゾーン中心モードであるため、Gバンドの振動数（~1580 cm−1）はレーザー波長に依存しません。Gバンドはすべてのsp2炭素材料（グラフェン、グラファイト、カーボンナノチューブ、アモルファスカーボン）に現れます。
Why "G"? The G stands for "graphite." This was the first Raman peak identified in graphitic materials, and the name has persisted even as the field shifted to graphene.なぜ「G」？ Gは「グラファイト (graphite)」の頭文字です。グラファイト系材料で最初に同定されたラマンピークであり、研究の主流がグラフェンに移った現在もこの名称が使われています。
02 — The 2D band02 — 2Dバンド

Double resonance at ~2700 cm−1~2700 cm−1 における二重共鳴

The 2D band (also called G′) is the most prominent second-order peak. It involves two iTO phonons near K with opposite momenta ($\mathbf{q}$ and $-\mathbf{q}$), produced through a double resonance process — making it strongly dependent on laser energy and uniquely sensitive to the number of graphene layers.2Dバンド（G′とも呼ばれる）は最も顕著な二次ピークです。K点付近の2つのiTOフォノンが逆向きの運動量（$\mathbf{q}$ と $-\mathbf{q}$）を持ち、二重共鳴過程を通じて生成されます。そのためレーザーエネルギーに強く依存し、グラフェンの層数に対して独自の感度を持ちます。

The double resonance process for the 2D band. An electron–hole pair is created near K, the electron is scattered by an iTO phonon to K′, then scattered back by a second iTO phonon, and recombines emitting a photon. Both phonon scattering events are inter-valley (K → K′ → K).2Dバンドの二重共鳴過程。K点付近で電子–正孔対が生成され、電子がiTOフォノンによりK′に散乱され、さらに2番目のiTOフォノンにより散乱されて戻り、光子を放出して再結合します。両方のフォノン散乱は谷間散乱（K → K′ → K）です。

Why is the 2D band so useful?
  • Layer counting: In monolayer graphene, the 2D band is a single sharp Lorentzian (~24 cm−1 FWHM) with intensity much larger than G. In bilayer, it splits into four components due to the splitting of electronic bands. In few-layer graphene, the shape evolves further — making the 2D lineshape a reliable fingerprint for layer number.
  • Dispersive: The 2D frequency shifts ~100 cm$^{-1}$/eV with laser energy because the resonant phonon wavevector $\mathbf{q}$ depends on the electronic band structure.
  • No defects needed: Unlike the D band, momentum is conserved by the two phonons ($\mathbf{q} + (-\mathbf{q}) = 0$), so the 2D band appears in pristine graphene.
なぜ2Dバンドは有用なのか？
  • 層数の同定：単層グラフェンでは、2Dバンドは鋭い単一ローレンツ関数（半値幅~24 cm−1）で、Gバンドより強度が大きくなります。二層では電子バンドの分裂により4成分に分裂します。少数層グラフェンではさらに形状が変化し、2Dバンドの線形が層数の信頼できる指標となります。
  • 分散性：共鳴フォノン波数ベクトル $\mathbf{q}$ が電子バンド構造に依存するため、2D振動数はレーザーエネルギーに対して~100 cm$^{-1}$/eV シフトします。
  • 欠陥不要：Dバンドと異なり、2つのフォノンにより運動量が保存される（$\mathbf{q} + (-\mathbf{q}) = 0$）ため、2Dバンドは欠陥のないグラフェンにも現れます。
Why "2D" and not "G′"? The 2D band involves two phonons of the same type as the D band (iTO near K), hence its frequency is roughly twice the D band frequency. Both names are used in the literature — "2D" emphasizes the phonon origin, while "G′" avoids confusion with implying defect involvement.なぜ「2D」であって「G′」ではないのか？ 2DバンドはDバンドと同種のフォノン（K点付近のiTO）を2つ含むため、その振動数はDバンドのおよそ2倍になります。文献では両方の名称が使われています — 「2D」はフォノンの起源を強調し、「G′」は欠陥との関連を暗示しない利点があります。
03 — The D band03 — Dバンド

Defect-activated double resonance at ~1350 cm−1~1350 cm−1 における欠陥活性化二重共鳴

The D band involves the same iTO phonon near K as the 2D band, but only one phonon participates — momentum conservation is completed by elastic scattering from a defect. This is why the D band is absent in pristine graphene and serves as a direct measure of disorder.Dバンドは2Dバンドと同じK点付近のiTOフォノンを含みますが、関与するのは1つのフォノンのみです。運動量保存は欠陥による弾性散乱によって完結します。このため、Dバンドは欠陥のないグラフェンには現れず、無秩序度の直接的な指標となります。

The double resonance process for the D band. An electron is excited near K, scattered to K′ by an iTO phonon, then elastically scattered back by a defect, and recombines. The defect provides the missing momentum — no defect, no D band.Dバンドの二重共鳴過程。K点付近で励起された電子がiTOフォノンによりK′に散乱され、次に欠陥により弾性的に散乱されて戻り、再結合します。欠陥が不足する運動量を提供します — 欠陥がなければDバンドは現れません。

Quantifying defects: The intensity ratio I(D)/I(G) is widely used to quantify defect density. For low defect concentrations, I(D)/I(G) increases with defect density. At very high defect concentrations, both peaks broaden and the ratio decreases — the sp2 structure itself is breaking down. The relationship follows the Tuinstra–Koenig relation: $I(D)/I(G) \propto 1/L_a$, where $L_a$ is the average distance between defects (crystallite size).欠陥の定量：強度比 I(D)/I(G) は欠陥密度の定量に広く用いられます。欠陥濃度が低い場合、I(D)/I(G) は欠陥密度とともに増加します。非常に高い欠陥濃度では両ピークがブロードになり比は減少します — sp2 構造自体が崩壊しているためです。この関係は Tuinstra–Koenig の関係式に従います：$I(D)/I(G) \propto 1/L_a$、ここで $L_a$ は欠陥間の平均距離（結晶子サイズ）です。
D′ and D+D′ bands. The D′ band (~1620 cm−1) is another defect-activated peak, but involves intra-valley scattering (within the same K point). The D+D′ combination band (~2940 cm−1) involves one iTO phonon (inter-valley) and one iLO phonon (intra-valley), also requiring defects.D′バンドとD+D′バンド。 D′バンド（~1620 cm−1）も欠陥活性化ピークですが、谷内散乱（同一K点内）を含みます。D+D′結合バンド（~2940 cm−1）は1つのiTOフォノン（谷間）と1つのiLOフォノン（谷内）を含み、これも欠陥を必要とします。
04 — Reading a graphene Raman spectrum04 — グラフェンのラマンスペクトルを読む

What each peak tells you各ピークが教えてくれること

A single Raman measurement encodes layer number, crystal quality, strain state, and charge doping. Here is how to extract each.1回のラマン測定で層数、結晶品質、ひずみ状態、キャリアドーピングの情報が得られます。それぞれの読み取り方を以下に示します。

Layer number層数

  • Monolayer: single symmetric 2D peak, $I(2D)/I(G) > 1$単層：対称な単一2Dピーク、$I(2D)/I(G) > 1$
  • Bilayer: 2D splits into 4 Lorentzians, $I(2D)/I(G) \sim 1$二層：2Dが4つのローレンツ関数に分裂、$I(2D)/I(G) \sim 1$
  • Few-layer: 2D broadens asymmetrically, $I(2D)/I(G) < 1$少数層：2Dが非対称にブロード化、$I(2D)/I(G) < 1$
  • Graphite: 2D has two components ($2D_1$, $2D_2$)グラファイト：2Dが2成分（$2D_1$, $2D_2$）を持つ

Defects & disorder欠陥と無秩序

  • $I(D)/I(G) \approx 0$: pristine graphene$I(D)/I(G) \approx 0$：欠陥のないグラフェン
  • $I(D)/I(G)$ increases: more point defects or edges$I(D)/I(G)$ が増加：点欠陥またはエッジが多い
  • D′ appears: confirms defect activationD′が出現：欠陥活性化を確認
  • $I(D)/I(D')$: distinguishes vacancy (~7), boundary (~3.5), sp$^3$ (~13) defect types$I(D)/I(D')$：空孔(~7)、粒界(~3.5)、sp$^3$(~13)の欠陥タイプを識別

Strainひずみ

  • Both G and 2D peaks shift under strainひずみ下でGと2Dの両ピークがシフト
  • Tensile: red-shift (lower frequency)引張：レッドシフト（低周波数側）
  • Compressive: blue-shift (higher frequency)圧縮：ブルーシフト（高周波数側）
  • G band splits under uniaxial strain ($G^+$ and $G^-$)一軸ひずみ下でGバンドが分裂（$G^+$ と $G^-$）
  • Slope in $\Delta\omega_G$ vs $\Delta\omega_{2D}$ plot: ~2.2 for strain$\Delta\omega_G$ vs $\Delta\omega_{2D}$ プロットの傾き：ひずみで~2.2

Doping

  • G band stiffens (blue-shifts) with both n- and p-doping
  • G band narrows due to Pauli blocking of phonon decay
  • 2D band shifts down for n-type, up for p-type
  • Slope in $\Delta\omega_G$ vs $\Delta\omega_{2D}$ plot: ~0.7 for doping
  • Distinguishes strain from doping via correlation plot
The strain–doping correlation plot. Plotting $\Delta\omega_G$ vs $\Delta\omega_{2D}$ separates strain and doping effects: pure strain follows a slope of ~2.2, while pure doping gives ~0.7. Real samples often show a combination — the data point's position relative to these two axes reveals both contributions simultaneously. This technique, introduced by Lee et al. (2012), is now a standard tool in graphene characterization.
05 — From 2D graphene to nanoribbons

How quantum confinement reshapes the Raman spectrum

Cut graphene into a narrow strip — a graphene nanoribbon (GNR) — and the Raman spectrum changes dramatically. The finite width quantizes transverse momenta, creates new low-frequency modes, and activates edge-related scattering. Raman spectroscopy can identify the ribbon width, edge geometry, and electronic character.

Why the spectrum changes

In 2D graphene, phonon wavevectors span a continuous Brillouin zone. In a nanoribbon of width $W$, the transverse component is quantized: only momenta $q_\perp = n\pi/W$ survive ($n = 1, 2, \ldots$). This is the same zone-folding picture as for electronic subbands — but applied to phonons. Each allowed $q_\perp$ "slices" the 2D phonon dispersion, producing a family of discrete 1D phonon subbands.

Radial breathing-like mode (RBLM)

The most distinctive new feature is the RBLM — a low-frequency mode (~100–600 cm−1) where the ribbon width oscillates in-phase, analogous to the radial breathing mode in carbon nanotubes. Its frequency scales inversely with width:

$$\omega_\text{RBLM} \approx \frac{3222}{W} \;\text{(cm}^{-1}\!\cdot\!\text{Å)}$$

This provides a direct, non-destructive width measurement — the narrower the ribbon, the higher the RBLM frequency. For a 1 nm ribbon, $\omega_\text{RBLM} \approx 322$ cm$^{-1}$; for a 5 nm ribbon, it drops to ~64 cm$^{-1}$.

This $\propto 1/W$ scaling is not just a fit — it follows from zone folding the linear (longitudinal-acoustic, LA) part of graphene's phonon dispersion. The hard-wall boundary selects $q_\perp = \pi/W$, so $\omega_\text{RBLM} = a\,\pi/W$ with a slope $a \approx 1025.6$ cm$^{-1}\!\cdot$Å set by the in-plane sound velocity (note $a\pi \approx 3222$, matching the formula above).2

Effective width: $W \rightarrow W + \delta W$

When the simple $\omega_\text{RBLM} = a\pi/W$ relation is compared to real spectra of atomically precise, bottom-up GNRs, the measured frequencies sit systematically below the bare-carbon prediction. The fix is to replace the geometric width by an effective width $W + \delta W$: the edge hydrogens — and, in functionalized ribbons, the alkyl/cove side chains — vibrate roughly in phase with the carbon backbone, adding mass and lengthening the breathing motion. This pushes the effective width up by a few Å and the RBLM frequency down.

$$\omega_\text{RBLM} \approx \frac{a\,\pi}{W + \delta W}$$

Verzhbitskiy et al. showed by combining Raman with ab initio simulation that the RBLM is sensitive not only to width but to edge modulation and functionalization, and that a full description of the cove edges and alkyl chains is required to reproduce the experimental peak positions, splittings, and intensities.1 The Empa group later calibrated the length/width dependence across entire AGNR families, confirming the zone-folded LA assignment and the need for this effective-width correction to match measured values.2

G band in nanoribbons

The G band persists but is modified by confinement:

  • The $E_{2g}$ degeneracy is lifted — the G band can split into $G^+$ (along the ribbon axis) and $G^-$ (transverse), separated by a few cm$^{-1}$
  • For very narrow ribbons (< 5 nm), both components shift and the splitting becomes resolvable
  • The splitting and shift depend on edge geometry (armchair vs. zigzag) and width

D band and edge activation

Edges act as line defects that break translational symmetry — exactly the kind of elastic scattering needed for the D band. In nanoribbons:

  • The D band is always present, even in defect-free ribbons, because every edge provides the momentum $q_\text{defect}$ for double resonance
  • $I(D)/I(G)$ scales as $\sim 1/W$ — narrower ribbons have a larger edge-to-area ratio, so the D band dominates
  • Edge type matters: armchair edges efficiently activate the D band (inter-valley scattering), while perfect zigzag edges do not (they lack the K→K′ momentum transfer). The D band intensity thus distinguishes edge chirality
  • The D′ band (~1620 cm−1), requiring only intra-valley scattering, is activated by both edge types

D peak and CH modes as zone-folding signatures

In atomically precise GNRs the picture sharpens: the D peak, the CH-related edge modes, and the low-frequency lines are not generic disorder bands but can be read off directly as zone-folded versions of graphene's phonon dispersion. Mapping the $\Gamma$-point phonons of an $N$-dimer ribbon back onto graphene ("unfolding" the GNR Brillouin zone) reproduces the discrete subbands, and the allowed $q_\perp = n\pi/W$ momenta pin down which graphene phonon each ribbon line descends from.3

  • The D peak retains its graphene-like dispersion (frequency shifting with laser energy), which the Empa study uses as a unique fingerprint that distinguishes genuine GNRs from amorphous or defective sp$^2$ carbon.2
  • The CH modes — in-plane bending and stretching of the edge C–H bonds (~1200–1300 cm$^{-1}$ and ~3050 cm$^{-1}$) — appear at positions fixed by the edge geometry, and serve as direct probes of hydrogen termination.
  • The sub-1000 cm$^{-1}$ region is the most structure-specific: it is especially sensitive to edge morphology and functional groups, so it acts as a structural barcode for a given ribbon family.1

2D band: still present, but changed

The 2D band does not require defects (two phonons conserve momentum), so it persists in nanoribbons. However, quantum confinement modifies which phonon wavevectors contribute to the double resonance, and the electronic density of states develops van Hove singularities that enhance specific resonance channels. The result: the 2D band shape and intensity change with width, providing additional confirmation of confinement effects.

Summary: graphene vs. nanoribbon Raman

Feature 2D Graphene Nanoribbon (GNR)
RBLM absent present — $\omega \propto 1/W$
G band single peak ~1580 cm−1 split into $G^+$/$G^-$
D band defect-activated only always present (edges)
I(D)/I(G) $\approx 0$ (pristine) $\propto 1/W$

Connection to carbon nanotubes. Rolling a nanoribbon into a tube adds periodic boundary conditions instead of hard-wall ones, giving the same zone-folding physics. The CNT radial breathing mode (RBM, $\omega \approx 248/d$ cm$^{-1}$) is the rolled-up counterpart of the RBLM. G band splitting into $G^+$/$G^-$ occurs in both systems, and the D band serves as a defect/edge marker in both. Understanding GNR Raman provides a direct bridge to nanotube spectroscopy.
